Sage Atri is a revered ancient Brahmarshi and one of the Saptarishis (seven great mind-born sons of Lord Brahma) in Hindu mythology. 
He is a prominent Vedic seer credited with composing numerous hymns in the Rigveda, notably the entire fifth Mandala.
Origin and FamilyBirth: Created from the mind, eyes, tongue, or ears of Lord Brahma during the cosmic creation.Wife: Anasuya, the epitome of chastity and virtue, daughter of Sage Kardama.
Children: Due to their intense penance, the Hindu divine Trimurti—Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva—were born to them as Dattatreya, Durvasa, and Chandra (Moon).
Role in Epics and VedasRigveda: The fifth book (Mandala) of the Rigveda is dedicated to Atri and his descendants, containing 87 sacred hymns.
Ramayana: Sage Atri and Mata Anasuya's hermitage in Chitrakoot was visited by Rama, Lakshmana, and Sita during their exile, where Anasuya famously counseled Sita.
Astrology: Ancient Indian astronomy associates the star cluster of the Great Bear (Ursa Major) with the Saptarishis, with Atri holding a principal position.

Vrishabha Gotra is a specialized spiritual lineage primarily recognized within Veerashaivism (Lingayat tradition) rather than the standard Vedic Saptarishi gotra system. 
In this tradition, it is associated with the divine celestial pair (Gotra Dampati) of Vrishabha and Bhadra, representing sacred cosmic energy rather than a direct patrilineal blood descent from an ancient Vedic sage.
